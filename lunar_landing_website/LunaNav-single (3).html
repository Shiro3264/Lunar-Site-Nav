Landing on the Moon is about more than location.

Future lunar missions, especially those targeting the Moon's South Pole, face challenging environmental conditions. Understanding sunlight availability and direct communication opportunities is essential when evaluating potential landing sites.

Solar visibility

Can the spacecraft access sunlight?

The Sun remains close to the lunar horizon near the South Pole. Craters, mountains, and long shadows can affect illumination and potential solar power availability.

Earth visibility

Can the spacecraft directly communicate with Earth?

Direct communication depends on Earth's position relative to the lunar horizon. Visibility conditions can influence command, control, and scientific data transmission.

The next frontier of lunar exploration.

The lunar South Pole is an important target for future exploration because of its unique environment and scientific potential. However, it presents several operational challenges.

  • Long shadows

    Low Sun angles stretch shadows across the terrain.

  • Complex terrain

    Craters and ridges shape what a site can see.

  • Low-angle sunlight

    The Sun stays near the horizon, affecting potential solar power.

  • Limited communication windows

    Earth may sit near or below the local horizon.

  • Permanently shadowed regions

    Some areas may never receive direct sunlight.

  • Scientific importance

    Potential water-ice resources make the region a scientific interest. Site suitability still requires dedicated mission analysis.

LunaNav helps users understand these considerations by showing the Sun and Earth relative to a site's local horizon.
